### American Historical Association (2025). Guiding Principles for Artificial Intelligence in History Education

**Source**: American Historical Association Report

**Abstract**:
> This committee recognizes that generative AI tools offer significant opportunities to improve teaching and student learning. At the same time, we respect the concerns expressed by history educators, many of whom feel overwhelmed, distracted, or frustrated by these technologies. While generative AI is undeniably powerful, it cannot replace human teachers.

**Related Research**: Research on history teaching theory and objectives
**Key Evaluation**:
## Page 1
### Core Philosophy
- The document's foundational principle that **"historical thinking remains essential in an age of AI"** provides a powerful anchor and support for history educators
- Reaffirms the importance and value of the historical discipline in technological transformation

## Page 2
### Critical Examination of AI Limitations
- Identifies significant challenges in AI-assisted research:
  - **Hallucinating data and references**
  - **Inherent biases in training data and outputs**
- Positions AI as an information source requiring **rigorous scrutiny** rather than an arbiter of truth
- Prevents blind technological acceptance through critical awareness

## Page 3
### Forward-Thinking Educational Approach
- Advocates for cultivating **"AI literacy"** rather than implementing bans
- Embraces technology integration while maintaining critical engagement
- Acknowledges AI's deep penetration into daily life and learning environments
- Promotes skill development around responsible and effective AI use

### Critical Examination of AI's Limitations and the Educational Imperative

- **The Pervasiveness of AI "Hallucinations":** The critical stance on AI is profoundly relevant. Current models, while powerful, are inherently prone to **"hallucinations,"** routinely fabricating sources and data. This constitutes a severe threat to the foundational integrity of historical research and education.

- **Amplifying a Pre-Existing Problem:** The pre-AI **"Kashin Silver Mine" incident**—where a user spent a decade forging a complex fictional history on Wikipedia—demonstrates how convincing a deliberate historical hoax can be. AI dangerously accelerates this problem, enabling the generation of similarly detailed, persuasive, yet entirely fabricated historical narratives in minutes.

- **The Necessary Stance: Critical Engagement:** Consequently, we must treat AI as the elephant in the room: it can no longer be ignored, nor can it be blindly trusted. Its outputs are merely **probabilistic reconstructions** from vast and potentially biased datasets, not verified facts.
