## 📚 Chinese Literature

### Chen Xi (2024). Generative Artificial Intelligence Empowers History Teaching: Value, Scenarios, Challenges and Responses

**Source**: "Teaching Monthly · Middle School Edition (Teaching Reference)" 2024 Issue 12, pp. 54-58

**Abstract**:
> The introduction of generative artificial intelligence into history teaching is not only a necessary response to national development strategies but also an inevitable requirement to adapt to curriculum reform and empower classroom teaching. Generative artificial intelligence is a double-edged sword—it can empower history teaching in areas such as developing teaching frameworks, designing student activities, enhancing teaching presentations, optimizing instructional language, constructing generative classrooms, and creating assessment questions, while it may also cause issues such as misinformation, technological dependency among teachers, challenges to the role positioning of history teachers, and deviations in value orientation.

**Key Evaluation**:
- **Page 4: Optimizing Instructional Language**
  > Leveraging generative artificial intelligence can breathe new life into the static texts of history textbooks. It transforms dry historical accounts into vivid narratives rich with detail and plot, and restores abstract causal patterns to the real choices made by individuals caught in the torrent of their times. What this approach awakens is not only students' curiosity about history but also their profound resonance with the human condition—allowing cold historical records to blossom with humanistic warmth, and truly revealing the charm and essence of history as a humanities discipline.

## 🌍 English Literature

### American Historical Association (2025). Guiding Principles for Artificial Intelligence in History Education

**Source**: American Historical Association Report

**Abstract**:
> This committee recognizes that generative AI tools offer significant opportunities to improve teaching and student learning. At the same time, we respect the concerns expressed by history educators, many of whom feel overwhelmed, distracted, or frustrated by these technologies. While generative AI is undeniably powerful, it cannot replace human teachers.

**Related Research**: Research on history teaching theory and objectives

### Lea, Simon & Fidler, Ailsa (2024). Artificial Intelligence (AI) in Primary History – Take CARE!

**Source**: "Primary History" Issue 98, pp. 44-51

**Key Evaluation**:
- **Page 3: CARE Framework - Highly Practical with Strong Guidance for Teacher Practice**
  > The article proposes the CARE framework—Collect, Ask, Review, Explore—which provides clear and safe steps for frontline teachers (especially non-specialist or novice teachers) to use AI. This framework emphasizes information gathering, precise questioning, critical review, and exploratory improvement, positioning the teacher as a "guide" for AI rather than a "passive recipient."

### Kinderberg, Björn (2024). ChatGPT-Generated and Student-Written Historical Narratives: A Comparative Analysis

**Source**: "Education Sciences" Vol. 14, Issue 5

**Key Evaluation**:
- **Pages 7–9: ChatGPT Excels in Narrative Quality but Lacks Emotional and Moral Dimensions**
  > The study clearly shows that ChatGPT outperforms student writing in terms of linguistic fluency, structural coherence, and the use of complex rhetorical devices. However, it is notably weak in "moral response stance" and "emotional expression."
  
- **Page 14: Proposes a Constructive Shift: From "Preventing Plagiarism" to "Guided Utilization"**
  > The authors offer forward-looking pedagogical recommendations: rather than attempting to eliminate the use of ChatGPT, it should be leveraged as a teaching aid.

### Chimbunde & Moreneg (2025). Reconfiguring History Teachers' Assessment Practices in the Context of ChatGPT

**Source**: "TechTrends" Vol. 69, pp. 607-617

**Key Evaluation**:
- **Pages 5–6: Challenges of ChatGPT in Assessing History Students**
  > Elaborates on four major challenges posed by ChatGPT in history assessment: plagiarism and academic integrity, diminished critical thinking, widening digital divides, and lack of human interaction.
